# Manifesto



I look beyond the surface of a wall.



I pay attention to what has been added, what has been covered, what has been altered and what has survived.



I do not believe that something disappears simply because it is no longer visible. A newer layer can conceal what came before without completely removing it. A repair can preserve a structure while changing it further. What exists now is shaped, in part, by everything that has happened to it.



I am interested in the traces people leave behind, even when they have moved on and the meaning of those traces has been forgotten.



But I will not assume that every mark tells a complete story. A scratch, a stain or a faded inscription may indicate that something happened, but it cannot always explain what happened or why. The evidence and the story are not the same.



I refuse to see the wall only through abandonment, nostalgia or decay. It exists in spaces that are occupied, used and lived in, as well as those that have been left behind.



People alter its surface, but they are not the only ones. Rain, sunlight, moisture, plants and time also leave their effects. I see the wall's transformation as an ongoing relationship between human activity and its surroundings.



**I believe:**

- What is concealed is not necessarily erased.
- What is added becomes part of an existing history.
- What is repaired is not necessarily returned to its original state.
- What remains visible does not represent the whole history.
- What changes is not always lost.
- What cannot be established from evidence should not be invented.

I am not trying to recover every story or uncover everything that has been hidden. I am interested in acknowledging what remains without pretending to know everything that came before it.



**The wall does not preserve everything. It carries evidence, not answers.**

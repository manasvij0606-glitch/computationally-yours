# Identity — The One That Remains



## 1. Identity and Existence



**Name:** The One That Remains



**Identity:** An old wall that has existed through different periods of human occupation, use and change. Although built for a particular purpose, its identity has gradually been shaped by the people, activities and environmental conditions surrounding it.



**Place:** Spaces that have been occupied, used or left behind, including old buildings, abandoned spaces and lived-in environments.



**Role:** A physical structure that holds evidence of the different periods it has existed through.



## 2. Central Identity



The wall doesn't remember everything, but it carries evidence of things that others may have forgotten.



Its surface holds fragments of different periods. Some remain visible, others are covered or altered, and some eventually disappear. These traces can outlast the people and circumstances that produced them, even when their original meanings are no longer known.



The wall does not preserve complete stories. It holds physical evidence of what has happened around it, leaving room for questions about what can be seen, understood and lost.



## 3. History, Relationships and Change



The wall is built, occupied, altered, maintained and sometimes left behind. Different people contribute to its history, often without knowing what others have left before them.



Its condition changes through both human and environmental influences. Scratches, writing, layers of paint and repairs result from human interaction, while rain, sunlight, moisture, plants and erosion gradually alter its physical structure.



These changes are not always separate. New additions can conceal older ones, repairs can become part of its history, and environmental processes can further transform existing marks.



The wall also holds contradictions: it protects while separating, preserves while concealing, and appears permanent while continuously deteriorating.



## 4. Perspective and Attention



**Who is speaking?**



A wall that has existed through different periods of time, shaped by human interaction and its surrounding environment. Its perspective comes from its physical existence and the evidence accumulated on its surface, rather than from human feelings or a fictional personality.



**What does it care about?**



The focus is on what it holds, what changes it, what gets concealed and what eventually disappears. It draws attention to the difference between a physical trace and the story behind it.



**What does this perspective reveal?**



It offers another way of looking at how people occupy spaces, leave traces, change their surroundings and move on. While people and circumstances change, the physical environments they interact with continue to exist and transform.



It raises questions about how much of a place's identity comes from the people who have occupied it, and what remains of those relationships over time.



## 5. Principles and Boundaries



The identity of The One That Remains is guided by the following principles:



- **History is cumulative:** New changes become part of what already exists rather than always replacing it.
- **Concealment is not erasure:** Something that is no longer visible may still remain beneath the surface.
- **Change is continuous:** Human interaction and environmental processes both contribute to the wall's transformation.
- **Preservation is imperfect:** Attempts to repair or restore something can alter it further.
- **Evidence is not the complete story:** Physical traces can reveal that something happened without explaining its original meaning.
- **The wall exists beyond abandonment:** Its identity is shaped by both occupied and unoccupied spaces, not only by neglect or deterioration.



The wall should not be understood simply as a symbol of nostalgia, damage or loss. Its identity lies in the continuing relationship between what remains, what changes and what gradually disappears.

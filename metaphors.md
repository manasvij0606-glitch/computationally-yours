# DAY 3

## Metaphor Exploration — The One That Remains

The purpose of this exploration is to investigate different ways of understanding the old wall as a metaphor. Each direction highlights a different aspect of its identity: the traces it carries, the histories it conceals, the people who have interacted with it, and the forces that continue to change it.

The metaphors are evaluated not only for what they could communicate, but also for what they might overlook or limit.

### 1. The Palimpsest

**What it could showcase:**

Different periods of the wall's existence coexist on the same surface. New additions cover older traces without completely removing them. It represents accumulation, concealment and the persistence of the past within the present.

**Why it might not work:**

It could reduce the wall to a simple idea of layered history, overlooking its relationship with nature, human occupation and its contradictions.

### 2. The Silent Witness

**What it could showcase:**

The wall remains present through different periods of human activity, holding evidence of events without knowing or explaining their meaning. It explores the relationship between the wall, the people who have occupied the space and those who have since left.

**Why it might not work:**

It could make the wall seem like a passive observer rather than something actively transformed by its surroundings.

### 3. The Archaeological Site

**What it could showcase:**

The wall contains buried histories, where uncovering one layer reveals traces of another period. It explores how the present surface holds evidence of what existed before it.

**Why it might not work:**

It could focus too heavily on the past and the act of excavation, rather than the wall's continuous transformation in the present.

### 4. The Boundary That Absorbs

**What it could showcase:**

The wall separates spaces while simultaneously receiving marks, influences and traces from both sides. It explores the contradiction between protection and separation, and how something that divides can also absorb what happens around it.

**Why it might not work:**

It could overemphasise the wall's function as a boundary and underrepresent its accumulated history.

### 5. The Geological Record

**What it could showcase:**

The wall becomes a record of time, shaped by weathering, erosion, accumulation and environmental change. Although human-made, it is continuously altered by natural forces such as rain, sunlight, moisture and plant growth.

**Why it might not work:**

It could make the human presence in the wall's history feel secondary, focusing more on natural processes than on the people who have interacted with it.

### 6. The Witness to Absence

**What it could showcase:**

The wall remains after people leave. Their marks, alterations and remnants become reminders of presences that no longer exist. It explores how a space can retain traces of people even when they are no longer there.

**Why it might not work:**

It could make abandonment the central theme, even though the wall also exists in occupied and lived-in spaces.

### 7. The Imperfect Restoration

**What it could showcase:**

Repair and preservation become new additions to the wall's history. Attempts to return it to an earlier state inevitably change it further, creating another layer of its existence.

**Why it might not work:**

It could narrow the project towards damage and repair rather than the wider relationship between people, time, nature and place.

## Initial Observations

These metaphors offer different ways of interpreting the same wall:

- History and accumulation: The Palimpsest, The Archaeological Site.

- Human presence and absence: The Silent Witness, The Witness to Absence.

- Space and contradiction: The Boundary That Absorbs.

- Time and nature: The Geological Record.

- Change and preservation: The Imperfect Restoration.

## Metaphor Evaluation & Shortlisting

After examining the seven metaphors against the original aspects of the wall - its accumulated traces, concealed histories, transformation, human connections, relationship with nature and contradictions - five directions were retained for further exploration.

## Refined Metaphor Shortlist

### 1. The Palimpsest

The wall as a surface where different periods of existence overlap, with newer traces concealing but not entirely erasing older ones.

### 2. The Silent Witness

The wall as something that remains present through human activity, carrying evidence without knowing the complete stories behind it.

### 3. The Boundary That Absorbs

The wall as something that separates spaces while continuously receiving influences from the people and environments on either side.

### 4. The Geological Record

The wall as a record of gradual transformation, shaped by weather, time, erosion and environmental conditions.

### 5. The Imperfect Restoration

The wall as something repeatedly repaired and preserved, with every intervention becoming another part of its history.

## Shortlisting Notes

- The Archaeological Site was set aside because its emphasis on excavation and discovery overlaps with the Palimpsest.

- The Witness to Absence was incorporated into the Silent Witness because it represents one aspect of the wall's relationship with human presence.

These five directions will be explored further to understand what each contributes to the identity of The One That Remains.

## Selected Conceptual Direction

The Palimpsest — The One That Remains

The wall is understood as a surface shaped by accumulated histories. Every addition, removal, repair and environmental change becomes part of its continuing existence. New traces alter what came before, but do not necessarily erase it completely.

This interpretation brings together the wall's relationship with time, human presence, nature, concealment and transformation.

The Silent Witness and The Boundary That Absorbs remain useful supporting interpretations, offering additional perspectives on the wall's relationship with people and its surroundings.

# DAY 4

## Computational Metaphor Exploration

The Palimpsest - The One That Remains

The next stage explores computational concepts that could express the wall's accumulation, concealment, persistence and transformation.

### 1. Accumulated Data / Layered Memory

**What it could showcase:**

A system that continuously collects and retains information from previous states. Each new addition becomes part of an existing history, allowing multiple periods to coexist.

**Why it might not work:**

It could become a simple layering exercise without communicating how different layers interact or affect one another.

### 2. Overwriting and Data Persistence

**What it could showcase:**

New information modifies or covers existing information without necessarily eliminating everything that came before. It explores partial erasure, concealed histories and the persistence of older traces.

**Why it might not work:**

It could become too focused on digital text, data replacement or glitch effects, losing the physical qualities of the wall.

### 3. Entropy and Information Decay

**What it could showcase:**

The gradual deterioration of information and structure. It explores fading, fragmentation, loss of legibility and how traces can persist even as their original forms disappear.

**Why it might not work:**

It focuses primarily on degradation, whereas the wall also accumulates, gets repaired and receives new marks.

### 4. Diffusion and Erosion

**What it could showcase:**

The gradual spreading, dispersal and transformation of material through interactions with its surroundings. It connects to moisture, stains, weathering and environmental influences.

**Why it might not work:**

It could make the project predominantly about natural deterioration rather than the interaction between human and environmental influences.

### 5. Signal Interference and Noise

**What it could showcase:**

Multiple signals overlapping and interacting, making individual pieces of information difficult to distinguish. It explores fragmented histories, interference and incomplete visibility.

**Why it might not work:**

It could become generic digital glitch art without retaining a meaningful connection to the wall's physical history.

### 6. Path Dependency / Hysteresis

**What it could showcase:**

A system whose current state and future responses are influenced by its previous states. It explores how existing damage, repairs and accumulated changes influence what happens next.

**Why it might not work:**

It could become an abstract simulation of cause and effect without retaining the physical identity of the wall.

## Initial Observations

Three directions initially stood out for further consideration:

- Overwriting and Data Persistence: The persistence of older traces beneath newer changes.

- Path Dependency / Hysteresis: How the wall's past influences its future.

- Signal Interference and Noise: How accumulated traces overlap and become difficult to interpret.

Diffusion and Erosion also offered a distinct way to explore the wall's relationship with environmental influences and was retained for further evaluation.

## Further Exploration

The four directions were taken forward for further exploration to understand how their underlying concepts could translate into computational behaviours.

### 1. Overwriting and Data Persistence

**What it could showcase:**

Older traces surviving beneath newer changes, becoming concealed, partially visible or resurfacing over time. Different traces could persist for different lengths of time.

**Why it might not work:**

It could remain focused on the relationship between older and newer layers without exploring how the wall's history influences its future.

### 2. Path Dependency / Hysteresis

**What it could showcase:**

Previous conditions influencing future changes. The same event could produce different outcomes depending on the wall's existing condition and history.

**Why it might not work:**

It could become an abstract cause-and-effect simulation without clearly communicating the wall's physical transformation.

### 3. Diffusion and Erosion

**What it could showcase:**

Changes spreading unevenly across a surface, with different areas responding differently to environmental influences and existing conditions.

**Why it might not work:**

It could focus too heavily on environmental processes and deterioration, overlooking human interventions and accumulated history.

### 4. Signal Interference and Noise

**What it could showcase:**

Overlapping traces becoming fragmented or difficult to distinguish, while still retaining evidence of their previous existence.

**Why it might not work:**

It could become focused on distortion and visual complexity rather than the physical history of the wall.

## Shortlisting Notes

- Accumulated Data / Layered Memory was set aside because its focus on accumulation overlaps with the Palimpsest and Overwriting and Data Persistence.

- Entropy and Information Decay was set aside because it focuses primarily on deterioration and loss.

- Overwriting and Data Persistence was retained for its exploration of persistence and concealment.

- Path Dependency / Hysteresis was retained for its exploration of how the past influences future changes.

- Diffusion and Erosion was retained for its exploration of environmental transformation.

- Signal Interference and Noise was set aside for now because its focus on interpretation and visual distortion offers a less direct connection to the physical transformation of the wall.

These three computational metaphors will be carried forward as independent directions for further generative exploration.
